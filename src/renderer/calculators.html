Ring blank length

Bent metal keeps its length along the middle of its thickness, so a band needs π × (inside diameter + thickness) of stock, plus a little to file the ends true for a tight solder joint.

Ring size conversion chart

Sizes in the chart are the usual equivalents; makers' charts differ by up to a quarter size.

Bangle or hoop cut length

The same arithmetic as a ring blank, for anything round made from stock: a bangle, a hoop earring, a large jump ring. Bangles are usually 60 mm (small), 65 mm (medium) or 70 mm (large) inside.

Bezel strip length

The stone's perimeter, plus π × the bezel's thickness because the outside of the bend travels farther than the inside, plus a little overlap for the joint. Ovals use Ramanujan's approximation.

Jump ring wire

Wire per ring is the circumference measured along the middle of the wire: π × (mandrel + wire). Rings spring back a little after cutting, so the inside ends up 0.1 to 0.2 mm larger than the mandrel.

Metal weight and cost

Volume of the stock times the metal's density. Alloys vary a little by maker.

Karat and alloy

Karat is gold in 24 parts: 18k is 18/24, or 75% gold. To lower the karat, add alloy; to raise it, add fine gold.

Gauge to mm chart

American Wire Gauge, also called Brown & Sharpe, is what jewellery suppliers in North America use for wire and sheet alike. A higher number is thinner. Each step is about 11% thinner; six steps halves the thickness.
